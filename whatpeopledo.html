What people do on Dia de los Muertos

Dia de los Muertos is day where you respect people who have died and keeping the memory of them alive.

  1. Building Ofrendas: Families create intricate altars in their homes or at cemeteries. These are decorated with bright yellow marigolds, candles, papel picado (cut paper banners), and the favorite foods, drinks, and personal items of the departed.
  2. Decorating Gravesites: People visit local cemeteries to clean, decorate, and spend the night at the resting places of their loved ones. Graves are illuminated with hundreds of candles and covered in flowers to help guide the spirits back.
  3. Dressing Up: Participants often wear skull masks, paint their faces to look like calaveras (skeletons), and dress in fancy clothing topped with large hats—a style inspired by the famous Mexican figure, La Catrina.
  4. Feasting: Special traditional foods are prepared, most notably pan de muerto (a sweet, egg-based bread) and decorated sugar or chocolate skulls.
  5. Day of the Little Angels: November 1 is specially designated as Día de los Angelitos, a day to honor and remember infants and children who have passed away, while November 2 is reserved for adults
